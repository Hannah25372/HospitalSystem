Question
The director of the largest hospital in the country (around 500.000 patients per year) wants to 
have an overview of the average age of the patients who visited the hospital, divided by sex 
and per month, for the past 10 years. 
He wants to see this information immediately 200ms.


Answer
At 500k patients a year for 10 years, this would be approximately 5 million stay records. A query to the 
database for this many records would not be within 200ms.

An aggregate table could be created which incrementally stores and updates this summary data when a new stay 
is created.

This table could be as follows:
PatientAgeSummary
- hospital_id
- month
- year
- sex
- total_combined_ages
- patient_count
composite key on (hospital_id, month, year, sex)

When a new stay is created, the PatientAgeSummary is queried to find the corresponding row which matches
to the month and year of the new stay, and the sex of the patient. 
The age of the patient in the new stay is added to the total_combined_ages and patient_count is 
incremented by 1.

When the director wants to view the described overview, this query can retrieve all records from 
PatientAgeSummary in the last 10 years. This will be 240 records. (10 years * 12 months * 2 sexes).
This will be a much faster query than 5 million records as stated above, and should complete in 200ms.

The average age can be calculated at runtime by doing the following:
sum(patient_age_summary) / sum(patient_count) for each subsections of years, months and sex.
